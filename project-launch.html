01
The challenge

Selling to people who don't trust marketing

Ophthalmologists are among the most scientifically rigorous buyers in healthcare. They make decisions through peer-reviewed evidence, clinical peer recommendation, and long procurement cycles involving ethics committees, hospital IT, and clinical directors. Traditional B2B marketing tactics — hero messaging, case studies, product videos — land like noise.

The task was to build a go-to-market system that felt like scientific communication, not marketing. Every asset had to be evidence-backed, every claim citation-ready, and every channel choice justifiable to a KOL who'd read the original paper.

Audience 01
Retina Specialists
Evidence-first. KOL-influenced. Conference-driven. Trust is built through peer citation, not brand awareness.
Audience 02
Pharma Partners
ROI-focused. Need real-world data validation. Procurement cycles of 6–18 months. Respond to regulatory and endpoint credibility.
Audience 03
Research Networks
Publication-driven. Value collaboration and interoperability. Open to innovation if it reduces their workflow burden.
02
Strategy

Evidence as the creative medium

I built the GTM strategy around a core insight: the FDA clearance wasn't just a regulatory milestone — it was the most credible marketing asset we had. I led the PR narrative around the clearance, positioning RetinAI as the category-defining AI platform for retinal disease management. Every subsequent touchpoint — website copy, conference abstracts, LinkedIn posts, webinar invitations — drew credibility from that anchor.

The content strategy was deliberately structured for both human and AI-powered discovery: structured data, semantic clustering of clinical terms, and a FAQ architecture designed to appear in AI answer engines (AEO) when clinicians or researchers searched for "retinal AI platform" or "AI AMD monitoring."

Activation

Conference as the primary growth engine

In ophthalmology, conferences are where deals begin. ARVO, EURETINA, AAO, ESCRS — these were not just brand moments but the primary pipeline generation channel. I developed the full conference activation playbook: booth strategy, abstract submission calendar, KOL speaker briefing packs, pre- and post-event email sequences, and live demo scripts optimised for 10-minute conversations.

05
Takeaways
1
Regulatory milestones are marketing assets. The FDA clearance was more credible than any brand claim we could have made. Treating it as a content event — not just a legal box — amplified our launch by months.
2
In conservative markets, distribution beats creativity. Being present at 12 conferences matters more than a beautiful campaign. The channel IS the strategy when buyers trust peer environments over digital media.
3
AEO is the new SEO for complex B2B. Structuring content for AI answer engines — not just keywords — meant we appeared in AI-generated research summaries months before competitors had even considered the channel.
4
Neuromarketing principles work in scientific contexts too. Cognitive load reduction, anchoring (FDA clearance first, price last), and social proof (KOL citations over brand claims) all applied — just with a different aesthetic register.
