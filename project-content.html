01
The insight

The search engine is no longer the only engine

By 2023, a meaningful and growing share of research-stage queries were being answered not by Google but by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and similar AI systems. The standard SEO playbook — keyword density, backlinks, page speed — was necessary but no longer sufficient. A new layer was needed: content structured for how AI systems retrieve, synthesise, and cite information.

I built RetinAI's content architecture from scratch to be native to both worlds: optimised for traditional SEO ranking signals and structured to be cited, summarised, and referenced by AI answer engines. The goal was that any clinician or researcher asking an AI "what is the best AI tool for retinal disease monitoring?" would encounter RetinAI in the answer — not just in search results.

02
The framework

Three channels. One coherent architecture.

Rather than treating SEO, AEO, and GEO as separate initiatives, I designed them as a unified content architecture. Every piece of content was planned with all three signal types in mind — traditional ranking, AI retrieval, and language-model-friendly structure (clear claims, attributable sources, FAQ structure, concise definitions).

Layer 01 · SEO
Search Engine Optimisation
Keyword clusters, topical authority, technical SEO, internal linking, and backlink acquisition through publication partnerships.
Layer 02 · AEO
Answer Engine Optimisation
Structured FAQ pages, definition-first content, schema markup, and source attribution designed to be retrieval-friendly for AI systems.
Layer 03 · GEO
Generative Engine Optimisation
Authority signals that LLMs weight: peer-reviewed citations, institutional co-authorships, and quoted expert statements that embed the brand in AI training data signals.

03
Content types

A full content ecosystem

The program was built as an interconnected ecosystem — not a collection of standalone assets. Each content type served a specific role in the buyer journey and was cross-referenced to maximise topical authority signals for both search engines and AI systems.

1
Clinical white papers
Long-form, citation-heavy documents co-authored with KOLs. Highest authority signal for both SEO and GEO — peer-reviewed adjacent content that AI systems weight heavily.
2
KOL interview series
Structured Q&A content with retina specialists and research leads. High social proof value and direct pipeline generation from within the clinical community.
3
FAQ & definition pages
Explicitly structured for AEO: clear question-answer pairs, concise definitions, and structured schema markup. Designed to appear as featured snippets and AI-generated summaries.
4
LinkedIn thought leadership
Personal brand content from executives and scientific leadership. Highest engagement rate and lowest CPL of any channel — organic community building inside the professional clinical network.
5
Pharma partnership series
Co-branded content with pharmaceutical companies leveraging RetinAI data. Cross-distribution into pharma audiences doubled the content's reach at zero incremental cost.
6
Conference abstract strategy
Planned submission calendar across 12 congresses. Each accepted abstract doubled as an SEO asset and an AEO authority signal when indexed by academic search engines.

04
Neuromarketing layer

What the brain does before the decision

Neuromarketing principles were embedded at the content architecture level — not as stylistic choices but as structural decisions. The goal: reduce cognitive load, increase trust signals, and make the "next step" feel effortless rather than deliberate.

Key applications across the content program: cognitive load reduction through progressive disclosure (summary → detail, never the reverse), anchoring the FDA clearance as the first trust signal in every new context, social proof sequencing (peer citations before product claims), and loss-aversion framing in pharma content ("the data you're missing" rather than "the data we provide").

06
Takeaways
1
AEO is not a future consideration — it's a present opportunity. Most brands are still optimising exclusively for Google. The gap between AEO-aware and AEO-naive content strategy is now large enough to be a genuine competitive moat.
2
Simplicity is the highest-ROI content decision. In technical markets, the instinct is to prove depth through complexity. The opposite is true: content that can be summarised by an AI in one clear sentence is content that will be cited by that AI.
3
Neuromarketing works at the architecture level, not the copy level. The biggest conversion gains came not from headline testing but from restructuring information flow — what comes first, what is implicit versus explicit, and how much cognitive work the reader has to do.
4
Earned authority compounds exponentially. Each publication partnership, KOL interview, and conference abstract made the next one easier to secure. After 12 months, inbound content collaboration requests exceeded outbound outreach — the system was generating its own growth.
